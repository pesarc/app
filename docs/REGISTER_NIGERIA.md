# Registering Pesarc in Nigeria — the cheapest sensible path

General information, not legal advice. Fees change; confirm on the official CAC
portal (<https://www.cac.gov.ng>) and take a lawyer for the licensing question at
the end. The goal here: spend the least while ending up with something a bank and
an investor will accept.

## TL;DR
- **Register it yourself on the CAC portal** — agents charge ₦50k–150k for what
  the portal does for a fraction of that.
- **Entity:** a **Private Company Limited by Shares ("Ltd")** is what you want if
  you'll raise money, open a corporate bank account, or get licensed. A
  **Business Name** is cheaper but is not a real company and won't serve fintech.
- **Rough DIY cost of a Ltd:** ~**₦25,000–40,000** all-in at ₦1,000,000 share
  capital (name reservation + filing + stamp duty). A Business Name is
  ~**₦10,000–25,000** but you'll likely re-register as a Ltd later, so paying
  once for the Ltd is usually cheaper overall.

## Which entity?

| | Business Name (Enterprise) | **Private Company Ltd (recommended)** |
|---|---|---|
| Cost (DIY) | ~₦10k–25k | ~₦25k–40k @ ₦1M capital |
| Separate legal entity | No | **Yes** |
| Corporate bank account | Hard | **Yes** |
| Raise from investors | No | **Yes** |
| Get a fintech licence | No | **Yes** |

Unless you're only testing a name, go **Ltd**.

## Steps (CAC portal, DIY)

> **The two dropdowns that confuse everyone:**
> - **Business Classification → `Company`** (not Business Name / Limited
>   Partnership / LLP). A "Company" is a real incorporated legal entity.
> - **Business Type → `Private Company Limited By Shares`** (not Unlimited, not
>   Public). This is the standard private "Ltd" — liability limited to your
>   shares; what banks and investors expect. Your name will end in "Limited".

1. **Create an account** on the CAC portal (pre.cac.gov.ng / the CAC company
   registration portal).
2. **Reserve the name** "Pesarc" (~₦500–1,000). If taken, try "Pesarc
   Technologies Ltd" / "Pesarc Financial Technologies Ltd".
3. **Fill the incorporation form:**
   - Directors + shareholders (you can be both; minimum **1** now allowed for a
     small company, but 2 is common). Valid ID + phone + email for each.
   - **Share capital:** keep it at **₦1,000,000**. Stamp duty is ~0.75% of
     capital, so a bigger number costs more for no early benefit.
   - Registered address (a home address or a cheap virtual office is fine).
   - Objects: payments / financial technology / software.
4. **Upload IDs**, signatures, and pay the fees.
5. Receive the **Certificate of Incorporation**, the status report, and a **TIN**
   (tax ID, now issued automatically with the JTB integration).

## Right after registration (mostly free)
- **Open a corporate bank account** (bring CoI, status report, TIN, directors'
  IDs). Free.
- Confirm your **TIN / FIRS** tax registration (usually auto). Free.
- Get a company **stamp/seal** if your bank asks (cheap).

## Cost-saving tips
- Do it on the portal yourself; skip agents.
- ₦1,000,000 share capital (don't inflate it — stamp duty scales with it).
- Home/virtual address instead of renting an office.
- One entity now; add trademarks/branch registrations later when funded.

## The part registration does NOT cover: fintech licensing (get counsel)

Company registration ≠ permission to run a payments/fintech business. Moving
money and dealing in stablecoins in Nigeria touches:
- **CBN** licensing for payments (e.g. PSSP / PSP / Switching / MMO), depending
  on your exact model.
- **SEC Nigeria** digital-asset / VASP rules for stablecoin-related activity.

Two things that lower cost and risk early:
1. **Stay non-custodial and settle through licensed partners.** Because users
   control their own funds and you pay out via a **licensed provider**
   (Paystack/Flutterwave et al.), you may operate initially under partners'
   licences rather than holding your own — much cheaper. Confirm this structure
   with a Nigerian fintech lawyer.
2. **Don't take custody of customer fiat** in your own accounts until you have
   the right licence; that's the line that triggers the expensive CBN regime.

Budget a **one-off consult with a Nigerian fintech/regulatory lawyer** (often a
few hundred dollars) before you touch customer money at scale. It's the cheapest
insurance you'll buy.
